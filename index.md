### 关于讲义/About the notes

古典平面几何既是一套值得深入欣赏的理论，也是一种训练直觉、推理与抽象能力的方式。《平面几何导论》是以综合方法为主线的几何讲义，面向具备高中数学基础、希望进一步理解平面几何的读者。它未必以竞赛技巧的积累或解题速度的提升为主要目标，而是更希望帮助读者建立较为系统的几何认识，理解不同观点之间的联系。

读者既可循序学习，也可依照前置知识的关系按专题选读。正文配有习题，部分习题附有提示或解答，并设有索引。讲义从熟悉的点、线、圆与三角形出发，将圆锥曲线作为基本研究对象较早引入，逐步展开近代欧氏几何与射影几何的理论，包括反演、等角共轭、交比、配极等工具。讲义将许多看似分散的经典命题联系起来，着重说明几何构造背后的共同原理。在古典几何的基础上，后续内容进一步介绍射影空间的结构与公理化观点；附录则补充并拓展集合与映射、代数、拓扑和基础分析等知识，为理解古典几何与现代数学之间的联系提供背景。

本讲义仍在持续修订与增补中，这里提供的是目前已经完成的部分。后续计划包括平面几何专题增补、高次曲线、平面几何的代数方法等内容。

讲义有中文与英文两个版本。欢迎读者提出勘误与建议，也可发送至 [rozen_F@outlook.com](mailto:rozen_F@outlook.com)。

Classical plane geometry is both a body of theory worth exploring for its own sake and a means of developing intuition, reasoning, and the capacity for abstract thought. *Introduction to Plane Geometry* is a set of lecture notes that approaches the subject primarily through synthetic methods. It is intended for readers with a background in high-school mathematics who wish to deepen their understanding of plane geometry. The notes may also be useful to readers interested in problem solving or maths competitions. They place greater emphasis on helping readers develop a systematic understanding of geometry and appreciate the connections between different perspectives than on acquiring competition-specific techniques or improving problem-solving speed.

Readers may work through the notes sequentially or study selected topics, consulting the necessary prerequisite material as needed. The text includes exercises, hints or solutions to selected exercises, and indexes for reference. Beginning with familiar points, lines, circles, and triangles, the notes introduce conics early as fundamental objects of study and gradually develop the theory of modern Euclidean and projective geometry, using tools such as inversion, isogonal conjugacy, cross-ratios, and polarity. Many seemingly disparate classical results are brought together, with particular attention to the common principles underlying geometric constructions. Building on this foundation in classical geometry, later chapters introduce the structure of projective spaces and the axiomatic approach to geometry. An appendix provides background in sets and mappings, algebra, topology, and elementary analysis, helping readers understand the connections between classical geometry and modern mathematics.

The notes continue to be revised and expanded; the material available here represents the work completed so far. Planned additions include further topics in plane geometry, higher-degree curves, and algebraic methods in plane geometry.
